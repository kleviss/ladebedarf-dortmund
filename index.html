Where Dortmund needs its next charging points.

ElectriCity runs the city's own calculation on the city's own data and shows, for every statistical subdistrict, how many public charging points exist, how many today's electric cars need, and where the new ones could go.

Candidate for a Stadt Dortmund pilot projectThe next step is testing the tool with the city's planners on real decisions about where to build.

Every charging point at the kerb takes street space that a cycle path, a wider pavement or a tree could have.

So Dortmund wants to build only where the need is real and can be shown. Today that answer is spread over spreadsheets, a consultant's report and a dozen datasets. ElectriCity puts it on one map, with every number traceable to its source.

… public charging points are missing at Dortmund's kerbs today, by the default model in the app, on top of … that already exist.

From a subdistrict to a site

  1. Pick a subdistrict

    Click it on the map or choose it from the list. The map zooms to its boundary and the details open beside it.

  2. See the gap

    Existing points, the points today's electric cars need, and what is missing, split into residents, people commuting in and visitors.

  3. Find the sites

    A ranked list of candidate sites in the priority area, each with a Street View link, so the kerb can be checked before anyone drives out.

  4. Move charging off the street

    Switch on supermarket car parks, buildings in the priority area and commercial car parks, and see how much kerb demand they take over. Each option only appears where the subdistrict has such sites.

  5. Take it to the meeting

    Printable reports cover before and after, priorities, parking, walking-distance coverage and data quality. Every setting lives in the link, so a colleague sees exactly the same view.

Built on the city's numbers, not ours

The calculation follows the city's ISME method from its December 2023 report, and the tests reproduce the cells of the city's own spreadsheet. Where a city-wide average hides real differences between neighbourhoods, the model uses that subdistrict's own data instead.

Bring ElectriCity to your city.

Nothing in the method is specific to Dortmund. It needs a city's vehicle stock per district, its charging locations and its parking and land use, which most German cities already keep.
